Self-RAG is a framework that trains a language model to adaptively
decide when retrieval is needed, to critique the relevance of
retrieved passages, and to check whether its own generated claims are
actually supported by those passages. It does this using special
reflection tokens that are learned during training rather than
inserted by hand at inference time.

The three main categories of reflection tokens in Self-RAG are:
retrieval tokens, which decide whether to retrieve at all; relevance
tokens (ISREL), which judge whether a retrieved passage is useful for
answering the query; support tokens (ISSUP), which check whether a
generated statement is backed up by the retrieved passage; and utility
tokens (ISUSE), which rate the overall usefulness of a response.

Self-RAG differs from standard RAG in that it can retrieve multiple
times during generation, skip retrieval entirely when it is not
needed, and select among multiple candidate generations based on their
critique scores rather than always accepting the first output.

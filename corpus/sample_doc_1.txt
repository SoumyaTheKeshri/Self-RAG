Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines a
language model with an external retrieval system. Instead of relying
solely on knowledge stored in the model's parameters, RAG retrieves
relevant passages from a document collection and includes them in the
prompt before generating an answer. This helps reduce hallucination
because the model can ground its response in retrieved evidence rather
than inventing facts from memory.

A typical RAG pipeline has two main stages: retrieval, where a query
is embedded and compared against a vector index of document chunks to
find the most similar passages, and generation, where the language
model produces an answer conditioned on both the query and the
retrieved passages.

One limitation of standard RAG is that it retrieves a fixed number of
passages for every query regardless of whether retrieval is actually
necessary, and regardless of whether the retrieved passages are
relevant. This can introduce noise into the generation process and
sometimes leads to less accurate or less useful answers.
